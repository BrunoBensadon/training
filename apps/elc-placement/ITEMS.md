# The items

Edition **2.0.0**: forty-eight statements, twelve sets of four,
one statement per preference in each set. They live in
`locales/<locale>/items.json` and are revised by editing that file — no
code change, no deploy pipeline knowledge required.

**Read "What I do not trust" at the bottom before using this with a real
group.**

## Where they come from

Edition 1 (six sets) was replaced after an evaluation found that it drew
the four preferences as quadrants rather than as stages of the cycle, and
that most of its "Activist" statements described *trying things out* —
the Pragmatist stage. See `docs/reviews/elc-placement-1-evaluation.md`.

The statements in this edition were drafted by an independent content
review (`docs/reviews/elc-placement-2-content-guide.md`, §3C), written
from the stage definitions below. None is reproduced, paraphrased or
adapted from Honey & Mumford's LSQ, Kolb's LSI/KLSI/KELP, the Honey–Alonso
CHAEA or the Felder–Soloman ILS; their item texts were not consulted.
What *is* borrowed is the model — the four stages of Kolb's experiential
learning cycle, and Honey & Mumford's reading of one preference per stage
— which is a published theoretical construct, not an instrument.

The Portuguese names (Ativo, Reflexivo, Teórico, Pragmático) match the
names used for the CHAEA in Brazil. This is not the CHAEA, and the about
page says so.

## The model

| Preference | On the circle | Step (shown) | Kolb's mode (about page only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active · Ativo | North | experiencing · vivenciar | concrete experience |
| Reflective · Reflexivo | East | reflecting · refletir | reflective observation |
| Theorist · Teórico | South | conceptualising · conceituar | abstract conceptualisation |
| Pragmatist · Pragmático | West | experimenting · experimentar | active experimentation |

The cycle runs clockwise. Kolb's "active experimentation" is the
**Pragmatist** point, not the Active one — the one naming trap in this
model, and the reason no Pragmatist copy uses the word "active".

## The rules every statement was written to

**What each statement must, and must not, express:**

| Preference | Must express | Must NOT contain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active (CE) | taking part, being in it, noticing what it is like, feelings, people, the moment | trying out, testing, improvising, deciding, planning, next steps |
| Reflective (RO) | watching, listening, reviewing, taking time, several viewpoints before concluding | explanations, principles, models |
| Theorist (AC) | explanation, principle, model, coherence, general cause | purpose or use; watching or waiting |
| Pragmatist (AE) | applying, testing whether it works, concrete change, planning use | asking for examples or stories, feelings |

**How each statement is written:** first person, present tense; 9–16
words, within ±4 words of the others in its set (the English length is
checked by a test); the same grammatical frame across a set; no
evaluative adverbs (*carefully, quickly*); no cost clauses; no key verb
shared across a set; one role per set; no idiom; gender-neutral
Portuguese.

The first edition deliberately added cost clauses ("even if the moment
passes") to make statements less flattering. This edition drops them: an
uneven cost is itself a desirability imbalance, and matching the
attractiveness of the four statements is the job of the pilot's
desirability ratings, not of wording that penalises one of them.

**Blueprint** — two sets per cell, role × phase:

| | Before | During | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| As a participant | 1 `new-activity`, 7 `new-idea` | 3 `sensitive-discussion`, 9 `role-play` | 5 `end-of-event`, 11 `what-did-you-learn` |
| As a trainer | 2 `new-topic`, 8 `co-trainer-disagrees` | 4 `unexpected-reaction`, 10 `own-mistake` | 6 `session-ended`, 12 `written-feedback` |

Sets are shown in this numbered order. The four statements within a set
are shuffled per respondent, so position carries no weight.

## The sets

Rank labels are 1 = most like me, 4 = least like me. English shown; the
Portuguese is in `locales/pt-BR/items.json`.

### 1. `new-activity` — participant, before
> A workshop begins with an activity I have never done before.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I take part straight away and notice what the experience is like. |
| Reflective | I watch how the first people do it before I join. |
| Theorist | I want to understand the idea behind the activity first. |
| Pragmatist | I want to know how I could use this activity with my own groups. |

### 2. `new-topic` — trainer, before
> I have to prepare a session on a topic that is new to me.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I start from a personal experience of the topic, or find a way to have one. |
| Reflective | I start by listening to people who see the topic in different ways. |
| Theorist | I start from the key concepts and how they connect to each other. |
| Pragmatist | I start from activities on this topic that have worked with other groups. |

### 3. `sensitive-discussion` — participant, during
> During a training, my small group starts discussing a sensitive topic.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I say what I feel and respond directly to what others share. |
| Reflective | I listen to everyone first and speak after I have heard the different views. |
| Theorist | I try to identify the general principle behind what people are saying. |
| Pragmatist | I suggest that the group focuses on what we could do about it. |

### 4. `unexpected-reaction` — trainer, during
> During a session I am running, the group reacts in a way I did not expect.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I stop following the plan and respond to how people are feeling right now. |
| Reflective | I pause for a moment and observe what is happening before I act. |
| Theorist | I try to understand what explains the reaction before I change anything. |
| Pragmatist | I switch to a different activity and check whether it works better. |

### 5. `end-of-event` — participant, after
> A training event is ending, and I think about what mattered most to me.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | What mattered most were the moments I experienced together with other people. |
| Reflective | What mattered most was what I noticed by watching and listening to others. |
| Theorist | What mattered most was understanding why some approaches to learning work. |
| Pragmatist | What mattered most were the tools and plans for my next session. |

### 6. `session-ended` — trainer, after
> A session I ran has just ended.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I think most about how the session felt, for me and for the group. |
| Reflective | I go through the session step by step to see what happened. |
| Theorist | I look for ideas about learning that explain why it went that way. |
| Pragmatist | I decide on one concrete change for the next time I run it. |

### 7. `new-idea` — participant, before
> Someone presents a new idea about how people learn.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I want to experience the idea in an activity, not only hear it described. |
| Reflective | I want some time to consider it before I form an opinion. |
| Theorist | I check whether the idea is consistent and well argued. |
| Pragmatist | I think about whether it would change anything in my next training. |

### 8. `co-trainer-disagrees` — trainer, before
> My co-trainer and I disagree about how to run an activity.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I say how I feel about each option and ask how my co-trainer feels. |
| Reflective | I ask my co-trainer to explain their view fully and listen before I answer. |
| Theorist | I compare both options with what we know about how people learn. |
| Pragmatist | I propose that we try each version once and compare the results. |

### 9. `role-play` — participant, during
> A training includes a role-play, and I am one of the players.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I let myself get absorbed in my role and in the situation. |
| Reflective | I keep part of my attention on observing how the group behaves. |
| Theorist | I try to identify the general principles the role-play is based on. |
| Pragmatist | I think about how I would adapt this role-play for my own groups. |

### 10. `own-mistake` — trainer, during
> I realise that I have made a mistake while facilitating.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I pay attention to how the mistake affected the people in the room. |
| Reflective | Afterwards, I review that moment in my mind before I draw any conclusion. |
| Theorist | I try to understand the general cause behind the mistake. |
| Pragmatist | I plan exactly what I will do if the same situation happens again. |

### 11. `what-did-you-learn` — participant, after
> Someone close to me asks what I learned at a training weekend.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I tell them about the moments I experienced and what they were like. |
| Reflective | I describe what I noticed about the group and about myself. |
| Theorist | I explain the main ideas and how they connect. |
| Pragmatist | I show them something I am going to do differently from now on. |

### 12. `written-feedback` — trainer, after
> I receive written feedback from participants about a session I ran.

| Preference | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Active | I pay most attention to the comments about how people felt. |
| Reflective | I read all the comments and compare the different points of view in them. |
| Theorist | I look for a pattern that explains the comments as a whole. |
| Pragmatist | I select the comments that point to changes I can make. |

## Reserve sets — pilot only, not shipped

Four more sets, written to the same rules, for a pilot build of sixteen
sets. After the pilot, the twelve sets that best meet the statistics in
the guide (§3H) ship and the rest go back here. They are deliberately
**not** in the locale files.

#### R1 `learn-a-method` (participant, before)
EN: A more experienced trainer offers to teach me a new facilitation method. | pt-BR: Uma pessoa com mais experiência em facilitação se oferece para me ensinar um método novo.

| tag | EN | pt-BR |
|---|---|---|
| active | I ask to experience the method myself as a participant first. | Peço para vivenciar o método primeiro como participante. |
| reflective | I ask to observe them while they use it with a group. | Peço para observar enquanto ela usa o método com um grupo. |
| theorist | I ask them to explain the principles that make the method work. | Peço que ela me explique os princípios que fazem o método funcionar. |
| pragmatist | I ask for the steps so that I can test it in my next session. | Peço os passos para poder testar o método na minha próxima sessão. |

#### R2 `choose-a-workshop` (participant, before)
EN: At a training event, I can join one of four optional workshops. | pt-BR: Em um evento de formação, posso participar de uma entre quatro oficinas opcionais.

| tag | EN | pt-BR |
|---|---|---|
| active | I choose the one where we live through a group experience, such as a simulation game. | Escolho aquela em que vivemos juntos uma experiência em grupo, como um jogo de simulação. |
| reflective | I choose the one where we watch recordings of real sessions and discuss what we noticed. | Escolho aquela em que assistimos a gravações de sessões reais e discutimos o que percebemos. |
| theorist | I choose the one where a speaker presents a learning model and the research behind it. | Escolho aquela em que alguém apresenta um modelo de aprendizagem e a pesquisa por trás dele. |
| pragmatist | I choose the one where we practise a technique and plan how to use it. | Escolho aquela em que praticamos uma técnica e planejamos como usá-la. |

#### R3 `plan-the-programme` (trainer, before)
EN: Our team is planning next year's training programme. | pt-BR: Nossa equipe está planejando o programa de formação do próximo ano.

| tag | EN | pt-BR |
|---|---|---|
| active | I share what participants and trainers experienced in last year's sessions. | Compartilho o que participantes e facilitadores viveram nas sessões do ano passado. |
| reflective | I listen to everyone's views before I give my own. | Escuto a opinião de todo mundo antes de dar a minha. |
| theorist | I propose an overall framework that links all the sessions together. | Proponho uma estrutura geral que conecte todas as sessões entre si. |
| pragmatist | I focus on what we can deliver with the volunteers we have. | Foco no que conseguimos realizar com as pessoas voluntárias que temos. |

#### R4 `watch-a-trainer` (participant, during)
EN: I watch another trainer run a session that is going very well. | pt-BR: Assisto a outra pessoa conduzindo uma sessão que está indo muito bem.

| tag | EN | pt-BR |
|---|---|---|
| active | I let myself take part in the session as if I were in the group. | Entro na sessão como se eu fizesse parte do grupo. |
| reflective | I note what the trainer does at each moment and how the group responds. | Anoto o que a pessoa faz em cada momento e como o grupo responde. |
| theorist | I try to identify the principles that make the session work. | Tento identificar os princípios que fazem a sessão funcionar. |
| pragmatist | I choose techniques that I want to use in my own sessions. | Escolho técnicas que quero usar nas minhas próprias sessões. |

## Translation notes, to confirm in cognitive interviews

- "dupla de facilitação" is used for co-trainer, to avoid a gendered noun.
- "dramatização" is used for role-play; "jogo de papéis" is an alternative.
- "formação" is used for training *events*, "treinamento" for a single
  training.
- Set 9 uses "Eu me deixo envolver" (standard proclisis with the pronoun
  subject).
- Copy says "o modo **Ativo**" or "o ponto **Ativo**", never "a
  preferência Ativo": the names are masculine and would otherwise have to
  agree with a feminine noun.

## What I do not trust

1. **Nothing here has been seen by a respondent.** No item-sort, no
   desirability ratings, no retest. The precision figures on the about
   page and in the guide come from simulation; the pilot (guide §3H)
   replaces them.
2. **Active may still be under-ranked.** The first edition's Activist
   statements carried more visible cost than the others; these were
   written to remove that, but a shift of only a quarter of a rank costs
   Active about ten points of share of top placements (guide F6). If
   Active falls below 15% of top placements across several unrelated
   groups, suspect the statements before concluding anything about
   trainers.
3. **Set 5 (`end-of-event`) has a different frame** — "What mattered most
   was…" rather than "I …". It is consistent within the set, which is
   what matters for ranking, but watch it in think-aloud interviews.
4. **Set 10, Reflective, starts with "Afterwards"** in a set about the
   moment of a mistake. Reviewing later *is* the reflective response, but
   it is the one statement in the set not set in the moment.
5. **The pt-BR terms in the translation notes above** are the ones most
   likely to read differently from the English.

## Revising an item

1. Edit `locales/en/items.json` and every other locale in the same commit
   — the build fails on key drift, which is deliberate.
2. Update the set above.
3. Versions carry meaning in saved links:
   - **Wording only** (the same set still asks the same thing): bump the
     minor number (2.0.0 → 2.1.0). Old links still show their result,
     with a note that the wording has changed.
   - **Meaning, number or order of sets, or the model**: bump the major
     number (2.x → 3.0.0). Old links then show "made with an earlier
     version" and are **not** re-scored, because their ranks belong to
     statements that no longer exist.
4. Follow `docs/CONTRIBUTING-TRANSLATIONS.md` for the translation itself.
